About

Where Continuum comes from

Continuum is a name for an old idea that never needed replacing: that what you own — and what you agree to — should be held by the parties themselves, and that connecting the world's chains should never require anyone to hand that sovereignty to a third party.

Our mission

Keep the satoshi sovereign

Our purpose is to defend a single property through every layer of abstraction: that what you hold — a coin, a deed, a title, a share of an escrow — belongs to the parties who hold its keys, and that an agreement is kept between them with no one in the middle. As value and contracts move across chains and through ever-cleverer machinery, that sovereignty must survive intact. When it cannot, the design is wrong — not the principle.

Our role

A lens, not a ledger

Continuum is not a chain, a token, or a company selling one. It is a philosophy — a way of judging designs by whether they honour the chains, the producers, the owners, and the counterparties to every agreement. Its job is to hold systems to Satoshi's standard and to give builders a clear line to build along.

Our values

Sound money, self-custody, honest consensus

We value what Bitcoin proved possible: a fixed and predictable supply, keys held by their owners, and a ledger secured by producers doing real work. We extend that same standard to every asset and every agreement — a deed, an escrow, a licence held as directly as a coin. And we reject the quiet substitutions: custodians dressed as contracts, a third-party signer slipped into a two-party deal, "trust-minimised" standing in for trustless. Continuity is a value here, not a limitation.

The creed

Seven commitments we build against

Short enough to remember, strict enough to argue with. If a system violates one of these, it is not Continuum — however clever it is.

Ownership is the premise, not the reward

An asset — a coin, a deed, a title — is held, not granted. No contract may take control of it and hand back a receipt.

Two parties, not three

An agreement is kept between the parties who make it. No external signer, arbiter, or admin key is added to a deal that only needs two.

Native to the chain, not a layer

Enforcement lives in the chain's own consensus. A rule that can be edited from an abstracted layer is a rule someone else controls.

Trust-minimised is not the goal

Reducing trust in a custodian is not the same as removing the custodian. We aim to remove it.

Every chain keeps its sovereignty

Interoperability connects equals. It does not fold independent chains into one machine that overrules them.

Producers are participants

The work that secures consensus is honoured, priced honestly, and never dismissed as mere plumbing.

Continue Satoshi; do not overwrite him

The goal was peer-to-peer value owned by its holder and exchanged with no one in the middle. We extend that goal — we do not replace it.

A community of people who refuse to give up their keys

Continuum is carried by builders, holders, and producers who share one instinct: that the point of this technology was never to trust a better machine, but to need less trust at all. They run their own nodes, hold their own assets, and read agreements before they sign them. They ask, of every new design, a single question — after this runs, do I still hold what I held, and does my agreement still bind only the people who made it?

That question is the whole discipline. It is why we care who orders the blocks, why we are wary of bridges and contracts that ask for custody, and why we treat "trust-minimised" as a warning rather than a promise. The community is not defined by a chain or a token. It is defined by that refusal, repeated in code, in writing, and in practice.
